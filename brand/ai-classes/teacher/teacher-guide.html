AI classes: teacher prep guide

Both courses, all three levels, week by week. For each week: the topic, what happens in class, the home task, what to prepare and what to watch out for. Tick the boxes as you prepare and use the notes lines after each class.

Every weekend: Sat + Sun, 1 hour each

Sat 0:00Welcome, recap last week, check home tasks (2–3 students share)
Sat 0:10New idea: explain with a live demo, a game or a question
Sat 0:25Guided build: everyone follows your shared screen step by step
Sun 0:00Quick recap, fix problems left from Saturday
Sun 0:10Own build or challenge; students share their screen when stuck
Sun 0:45Show and tell, give the home task, note one line for parents

Before every class

  • Build this week's project yourself, end to end, on a normal laptop (and on a phone for Level 1)
  • Send the Google Meet link and starter files on the WhatsApp group the day before
  • Check your mic, camera, screen share and internet; keep a phone hotspot as backup
  • Keep a backup: screenshots or a screen recording of the build
  • Step sheet for the guided build, shared as a PDF or Google Doc
  • Attendance, and a shared Drive folder where students upload their work (name_week.ext)
  • After class: short WhatsApp update to parents (what we did, home task, no student faces)

Safety rules, always

  • Classes 6–8: students never log in to AI chatbots or image tools. Only you type, on your shared screen.
  • Never type a student's name, school or photo into any AI tool.
  • No recordings or screenshots of student faces unless parents agree in writing.
  • AI is for learning and practice. If you see copied homework, talk to the student, not the class.
  • Never promise jobs, placements, marks or admissions.
  • Classes 9–12: AI accounts and GitHub only with parent permission (both need age 13+).

Classes 6–8 · Make with AI · Level 1 · Basics · ₹2,000 · 8 weeks

Discover AI

Goal: Understand what AI is, how it learns and how to use it safely.
Students build: A picture-recognising AI model and a first Scratch game.

Before Week 1

  • Ask parents to check that quickdraw.withgoogle.com, teachablemachine.withgoogle.com and scratch.mit.edu open on the child's device
  • Each child needs a webcam (laptop or phone) from Week 2; ask parents to allow camera access in the browser
  • Share the Scratch offline app link with families who have slow internet
  • Your own AI chatbot logged in on your laptop only; no student accounts
  • Students on phones: Scratch is hard on a small screen; ask parents for a laptop or tablet from Week 4

Tools: Quick Draw, Teachable Machine, Scratch. Phone or computer.

Week 1What is AI? Spotting AI around us: maps, YouTube, voice assistants

In class

“AI or not AI?” game, then Quick Draw on your own device

Home task

Find 3 places AI is used at home and tell a parent

My notes

Prepare

  • 12 “AI or not AI?” examples on slides or cards (workshop slides 2–6 work)
  • Quick Draw tested on a phone and a laptop
  • Short screen recording of Quick Draw as backup

Watch out: If a child's internet is slow, share your screen and let them call out what to draw.

Week 2How computers learn from examples: train a model to recognise pictures

In class

Train Teachable Machine to recognise objects, and see what bad examples do

Home task

Draw 5 things for a family member to guess

My notes

Prepare

  • Train a demo model yourself on 3 objects (pen, bottle, book)
  • A second “bad” model with only 5 pictures from one angle, to show failure
  • Webcams tested

Watch out: Students record too few pictures from one angle. Push for 50+ per class and a “nothing” class.

Week 3Talking to AI: asking clear questions and checking the answers

In class

Students write questions, the teacher types them and shares the screen; compare unclear and clear questions, catch a wrong answer

Home task

Write 3 clear questions on a subject you are studying

My notes

Prepare

  • 6 prompt pairs ready: unclear vs clear
  • 1–2 questions where AI gives a wrong answer (test beforehand, e.g. counting letters in a long word)
  • Textbook page to check the answer

Watch out: Only you type. Never put a student's name or school into the chatbot.

Week 4Scratch basics: characters, movement and loops

In class

Move a character, add sounds, use loops

Home task

Make a character dance to music

My notes

Prepare

  • Scratch starter project with 2–3 sprites
  • A finished “dance” demo
  • Block cheat sheet: motion, looks, sound, repeat, forever

Watch out: Kids spend ages picking sprites and costumes. Give a 5-minute limit.

Week 5Make your first game in Scratch

In class

Build a “catch the falling object” game with a score

Home task

Add one new idea to your game

My notes

Prepare

  • Build the catch game yourself: falling object, basket, score variable
  • Step card as a PDF
  • Starter file with sprites in the Drive folder

Watch out: Save every game as name_week5.sb3 and upload it to the Drive folder, or it is lost.

Week 6AI for studies: flashcards and quizzes the right way

In class

Make flashcards and a quiz from a chapter (the teacher types), then check them against the textbook

Home task

Quiz a parent with your flashcards

My notes

Prepare

  • Pick one NCERT chapter each for Classes 6, 7 and 8 (science or SST)
  • Textbook pages to check AI answers
  • Blank flashcard sheets

Watch out: Make sure the class sees at least one AI mistake and fixes it with the textbook.

Week 7Online safety: privacy, deepfakes and fake news

In class

Real or fake picture game, privacy rules, checking fake news

Home task

Teach your family the 5 golden rules

My notes

Prepare

  • 8–10 pictures: half real (free stock), half AI-made (make them beforehand)
  • 2 harmless fake news examples
  • 5 golden rules poster

Watch out: No pictures of real people or politicians. Keep it light, not scary.

Week 8Mini project and showcase for parents

In class

Finish your game and present it to parents

Home task

None. Celebrate!

My notes

Prepare

  • WhatsApp invite to parents one week before, with date and time
  • Showcase order list (1–2 minutes per child)
  • Short parent feedback form (Google Form)
  • Level 2 details: start date and fee

Watch out: Every child presents, even shy ones (let them present with a partner). No screenshots of faces without written permission.

Classes 9–12 · Build and Study with AI · Level 1 · Basics · ₹4,000 · 8 weeks

AI for Study and Life

Goal: Use AI to study better and honestly, and know where it goes wrong.
Students build: A personal AI study system: revision plan, question bank and flashcards.

Before Week 1

  • Written parent permission for students to use a free AI chatbot (they must be 13+)
  • Intro form: each student's board, subjects and goals (boards, JEE, NEET)
  • NCERT PDFs for their subjects (ncert.nic.in)
  • A Google account for Docs and Sheets; phones are fine for this level

Tools: A free AI chatbot (with parent permission), NCERT books, Google Docs and Sheets. Phone or computer.

Week 1How AI and chatbots really work, and why they make mistakes

In class

Next-word guessing game, then catch AI making mistakes

Home task

Find one wrong AI answer and the correct source

My notes

Prepare

  • Next-word guessing game: 5 sentences with a blank
  • 3–4 prompts where AI makes mistakes (test beforehand)
  • Intro form

Watch out: The goal is “check, don't blindly trust”, not “AI is useless”.

Week 2Asking AI good questions: clear prompts that get useful answers

In class

Role, task and format: rewrite weak prompts into strong ones

Home task

Make your own list of 5 study prompts

My notes

Prepare

  • 5 weak prompts from real student needs
  • Prompt formula card: role + task + context + format
  • Shared doc for their prompt library

Watch out: Show “quiz me on this” vs “give me the answer”. Ban “do my homework” prompts.

Week 3AI for board exams: revision plans and practice questions

In class

Make a revision timetable up to exam day, and practice questions from your syllabus

Home task

Follow the plan for one week

My notes

Prepare

  • Real exam dates (board date sheet when out) and syllabus PDFs
  • Google Sheets timetable template

Watch out: AI plans are too ambitious. Add buffer days, sleep and school hours.

Week 4AI for JEE and NEET concepts: explain, then verify with NCERT

In class

Get a hard concept explained step by step, then check every step against NCERT

Home task

Explain one concept to a friend without AI

My notes

Prepare

  • 3 hard concepts (e.g. electromagnetic induction, mole concept, meiosis)
  • NCERT pages for each
  • One AI explanation mistake found beforehand

Watch out: You are teaching a study method, not the subject. Tell them to confirm with their school or coaching teacher.

Week 5Research and fact-checking: sources and academic honesty

In class

Compare sources, cite them, and learn the rules on copying and plagiarism

Home task

Fact-check one viral message

My notes

Prepare

  • One harmless viral forward to fact-check
  • Fact-check sources (e.g. PIB Fact Check)
  • Example of how to cite a source

Watch out: Avoid political or religious forwards.

Week 6Notes, summaries and flashcards that help you remember

In class

Turn a chapter into short notes and flashcards, and plan spaced revision

Home task

Make flashcards for one chapter

My notes

Prepare

  • One chapter PDF
  • Flashcard tool (Google Sheets is enough)
  • Spaced revision calendar template

Watch out: Notes must be in their own words. AI only checks what they missed.

Week 7Safety: privacy, deepfakes and scams

In class

Privacy settings, spotting deepfakes, common scams like OTP and fake job offers

Home task

Show your family how to spot one scam

My notes

Prepare

  • Real scam examples: OTP, fake job, loan app, lottery
  • Deepfake examples from news
  • Privacy settings walkthrough for WhatsApp and Instagram

Home task
